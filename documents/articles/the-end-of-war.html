The end of war

 

People think conflict is inevitable, therefore war is inevitable. But with Open Source Governance it becomes possible to discuss the end of war itself. It all boils down to how wars happen.


Establishing an alternative

For over 20 years people have been trying to use the net to connect everyone and democratize the world. But every time they make a new system, everyday people basically just see it as some strange government website, and no one uses it. Sadly websites like this can’t offer users immediate gratification, which is what websites need in this day and age to attract lots of users. Which means even if someone designs the best system possible for humanity, it will almost certainly fail.

Which is terrifying because the more you study the threats facing humanity, the more you can see they’re the result of profit, and that fixing them will be extremely unprofitable. Even worse, politicians and CEO’s have entire communities of investors and analysts examining their every move, and will be replaced if they step out of line. They truly do not have the power to save us, even if they desperately wanted to.

So let’s begin with something manageable. What’s the simplest way to help one person?

Ask them what’s wrong, and listen until you have a good understanding of their problems. From there you want to empathize, and connect them with the best solutions possible. 



So a public archive or ‘wiki’ for all possible solutions, to any problem, with anything, is likely to truly help large numbers of people. -Even if they’re the only ones in their entire nation using it. Unlike ‘governance websites’, there’s immediate gratfication for anyone who’s got a problem and wants to see all their options side by side.



If we then embed voting features into a website like this, (a “solution wiki”), then suddenly we’ve got a platform for net-democracy that could actually attract millions of people.

And in case this all sounds oddly specific, yes, this is already in development. It’s name is Needpedia.org and it’s almost done. That means it needs content, testers, developers, artists, promoters, donations, and all kinds of other support. 


Important new abilities.

Had someone told peasants a printing press was going to dethrone kings, they would have thought the messenger was completely insane. Books can’t stop armies.

But books and fliers changed people’s access to information, and that changed everything.

Now that we have the net we know it’s possible to find solutions to anything, theoretically, but we also know it’s profitable for companies like Google to sell access to the “front page” of any given internet search. And they can do a lot more harm than directing you to a more expensive option, they control your access to all information. Google it.

*that’s not a joke it’s an illustration of how serious this problem truly is.

So in a sense what we need is a return to serving the needs of people. If they have a problem, they want to know what their options are. Whether that’s back pain or a problem they’re having at work. Or a problem they’re having with an oppressive government, or corporation.

Are you seeing a pattern here? The new kings have maintained power by controlling the flow of information, so to topple them we need to take the power (to access information) back. Let’s talk about how Needpedia’s designed to enable people to share ideas to fight systems of oppression:


2 important innovations

The Layer System. If you had an idea for something like a homeless shelter, experts in various fields are free to create new layers to your post, each one devoted to their own field or experience. Laypeople can even create layers for experts, then send experts links to layers to invite them into the discussion. If an idea accumulates enough support, a citizen-expert-coalition forms. In fact, Needpedia itself is, more than anything else, a catalyst for the creation of citizen-expert-coalitions.

To fully appreciate what Needpedia’s Layer System is capable of you have to realize there’s actually 2 different kinds of experts.

There’s the “experts” most people think of, (scientists, architects, doctors… ect), and then there’s all the people who are experts in what it’s like to be whatever race, gender, or other circumstance they know personally. That means if you post an idea for a homeless shelter and forget to think of accessibility for disabled people, they can create their own layer to your idea-post, then invite their friends to it.

This also means you yourself could create a layer for people with experience being homeless, then share a link to that with people and groups which have people with related experience. -Then you could collaborate directly with the community you want to serve. Compare that to how city’s do things now and you can see why citizen-expert-coalitions could be so invaluable.

Note that homeless people are also free to share their own ideas for a shelter, and their ideas might be better than yours. Especially if they’ve spent a lot of time thinking about it.

Ultimately, the goal is to have the people who are affected by decisions, being guided by the experts, to make the best possible decisions. That’s it, that’s the ideal decision making unit. -the same way a straight line is the shortest distance between two points. Politicians are simply an outdated information processor, invented before the age of pencils, and toilette paper. (No really, look it up. It is distressingly true.)

Conflict resolution language layers can be used to bring the cutting edge in conflict resolution psychology to the masses. This itself may prove to be the single most important aspect of Needpedia, because in the end, understanding problems are really just needs which aren’t being met, (mere logic problems), has the power to change everything. A single conflict resolution expert could change the way entire populations see a problem, in a matter of minutes. And people who want to avoid arguments or ‘negative energy’, can simply visit different post’s NVC layers, where a bunch of other people also went to avoid drama, and get results. Imagine national discussions happening in layers devoted to conflict resolution languages. 

The Token System allows users to drop tokens into pages, after any word they want, to ask questions, leave notes, or start debates. -All without changing the post anymore than adding small icons others can click on if they want to. This makes it easy for people to publicly expose misinformation, myths, and fallacies. -And answer questions. 



For those interested in solving problems, the debate token is especially valuable, because they’re designed to let you see which of your opposition’s arguments, individually, got the most upvotes. They let you see precisely which arguments matter the most to both sides. So that people can propose smarter compromises.



“Why not just elect scientists and other experts?”

Corruption. Once there’s billions of dollars on the line, certain people stop taking “no” for an answer. Whether they have to fund your opponent, or threaten your family, they will stop at nothing. Placing that much pressure, on an elite few, is like trying to design a spaceship using cardboard. It’s simply not the right material, for the job. Concentrations of power inherently lead to corruption. Therefore a symbiosis between experts and the public is absolutely critical for a functioning democracy.



In the past it’s always been possible for citizens to collaborate with experts, the same way it was technically always possible for someone to make a book. -but citizen-expert-coalitions in the past were typically very disorganized, and completely inaccessible for most of the people who they affected. -let alone everyone else who might want to learn from them.

But on Needpedia they’ll be far more organized, and their coalition building efforts will be far easier. Having your idea posted in the encyclopedia for solutions (and proposals) means that if people like it, they’ll share it and flock to it on their own. And if enough people like it, experts will start taking interest, and can easily be invited to the discussion with links from supporters. 



Which is critical because it means we can begin to form something that is far more powerful than existing forms of social organization. This is the part where we build the new thing that makes old thing obsolete. Fighting or burning down the old system isn’t good enough, we need to prove there’s a better way, or people will just keep recreating political systems they’ve seen before. Needpedia is a mass optimization engine, it has no democrats or republicans, just ideas, and people.

You can also think of Needpedia as a body of people, for instance, the Token System serves as antibodies for misinformation and myths. Needpedia also has a nervous system, you can also activate tracking on pages to get notifications about changes, and use them to help us maintain content quality, for content that’s important to you. 



Needpedia’s ‘body’ metaphor is especially apt because it also has the ability to evolve. It’s a wiki for ideas, for anything, so if you have any ideas for it itself, or any of its tools or features, then it’s easy to share them. For everyone. Which means we’re going to use it to just keep improving it, our (open source) programmers can even use the Layer System to collaborate directly with users, and vice versa.


End game scenario

So how do we end war? Simply put, we ban it. Or optimistically, we’ve already banned it in our terms of service, open source governance just haven’t taken over yet.

The moment we manage to form agreements as citizens, our elected officials can either work with us, or look bad. And to fully appreciate this you have to understand how this is just going to keep clawing away at their legitimacy over time. Because politicians aren’t going to get better, but our agreements and technology are.

Needpedia lets you list problems with all governments, corporations, and politicians too; so it’s going to cause enormous problems for corrupt people and groups. 



Elites can’t bribe or threaten the public, so the moment we make the public become the new decision making body, we make it impossible for corruption to function in most forms. All that’s left are disinformation campaigns, and the token system’s already designed to deal with those. -And even if they find some clever new trick to play on us, we can literally just add it to the list of problems with Needpedia, and collaborate on new solutions. And realistically speaking there’s only so much they can do before the public gets outraged.



Once we’ve proven our new system works, we have a few options. One is to just integrate it into the existing one. We first perfect our own ability to come together over ideas and form agreements, then use what we’ve created to make the act of working against our more popular agreements akin to political suicide. The lobbyists can only do so much for a candidate before they have to ditch them for someone more popular. Everyone collectively realizing there’s a bunch of stuff we can all agree on, and that new technology is here to empower them, would ignite a potent new global movement.

It would be lovely if it was that simple, but the sad truth is people cling to power. Many of them know they’ve committed crimes and will never let themselves become accountable.

So one thing we could do, is crowdsource support for new candidates who, are eager to work with us, and get our help winning elections. Then pressure them into using our ideas more and more until we’ve created so many new tools for accountability and representation, that the system actually works. This is unthinkable under todays system, but with the right technology, a simple bar becomes a lever.


The funniest thing about this method is that it sets the stage for the next, more innovative method.

If the public reaches agreements they really like, and politicians ignore them, then the public’s going to be furious with their “representatives”. And that emotion is precisely what sets the stage for mass protest and direct action.

Because governments promised us democracy and progress, and if we’ve already proven to the entire world that not only are they not going to give it to us, but that our method actually works better without them, then we’re left with a fight, but at least now we have an effective way to organize, and to replace the problem once and for all.


Ideally citizen-expert-coalitions would ban together around the creation of an “Open Party” and force their way into power nonviolently.



Open Party

A key benefit to creating an Open party is that communities would be able to nominate their most trusted members to represent them in government.



Meaning, these ‘reps’ would no longer have to spend money campaigning, because there would already be a community who promotes for them. This eliminates the need to sell out to corporations for campaign money. Open Party reps could have influence, and integrity. They would be experts at uniting factions around real-world solutions.

Another critical tool would be legal contracts with the open party reps. Having a legally definable entity (Needpedia) that can produce numerically quantifiable numbers, means that we can begin working on 2 extremely important things: 



1: The Open party rep’s automatic resignation for any violation of a community agreement. (followed by an emergency session where a new Open party rep is chosen). -Communities can stipulate whether that’s a 60% or a 90% consensus, and can customize other details. The key takeaway here is that being able to remove “representatives” for doing wrong is a very important ability. Almost as important as the ability to make agreements in the first place, because this is how we begin political implementation, this is how we begin taking the power back. It starts with accountability, contracts that make violators leave.



2: The next step is gaining the ability to undo violations. The idea of making someone resign if they violate a legal clause isn’t that far fetched, but realistically speaking, making a legal contract that undoes whatever votes or other legal actions a representative has already taken in office, would be much harder. But if we can accomplish that, then we’ll have politicians we can actually count on, because then violators aren’t just removed, their violations are removed with them. Suddenly lobbyists know that it’s not only potentially impossible to corrupt an Open party rep, but that even if they could, their powers would be decimated in the face of citizen expert coalitions empowered by Needpedia’s technology. And there’s only so many cheap tricks businessmen can pull before it will be obvious that once ideas hit Needpedia, they’re forced to compete with the best ideas on Earth.

And no one who’s trying to exploit the public is going to be able to win against everyone else’s ideas, in a fair fight. Our job as Needpedians is to make that competition as fair and informed as possible. So that the exploiters always lose, and the public will finally live free. -This time not from kings but billionaires.



Ending War

As mentioned before, proposing acts of war are already banned on Needpedia, but it’s important to note that once a nation managed to officially govern through technology like this, every other nation on the planet would automatically know they don’t need to arm themselves against an attack from them. It is effectively an automatic declaration of peace. The ‘war creation’ process gets eliminated. And the more countries that do it, the closer we get to a world where war as we know it becomes functionally impossible.

That’s not to say there won’t still be conflicts, but the scale and frequency of the violence could be 10-1000,000 times lower. -The difference between a gang fight and a military conflict is enormous.

Certain governments would inevitably cling to power, and with them mass oppression and the potential for war, so it’s important to also consider them. 



The first thing we need to do is get our own house in order. Right now dictators all over the world are pointing to the dysfunction in democracies like the US, and are actively using it to validate dictatorship. So the first thing we have to do is scare them by doing what none believed possible: we have to prove democracy can work, and that it’s awesome, (wildly outperforms its competition). There’ll be videos of us celebrating victories, but also posts about the ideas that worked. Those ideas will spread, and our enthusiasm with them. A smart dictator might use them, but inevitably their empire of exploitation will be revealed, so it might take international coalitions to pressure them. Assuming their own internal flaws don’t leave them crumbling the moment their citizens realize other people have it way better. (their leaders can no longer point to our dysfunction and claim authoritarianism is best)


The bottom line is this: our govs don’t work, our planet is dying, and we need to make something that does work. And what better way to do that than creating an archive for all the best ideas on Earth?
