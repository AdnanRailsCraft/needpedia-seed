Potential Collaboration Modes: CELTC & Needpedia

A straightforward list of ways we can work together.

1. The Experiential Learning Lab

Use Needpedia as the primary platform for student projects in your Transformative Leadership and Sustainable Co-op Development certificates.

  • How it works: Students manage their real-world projects on the platform—posting ideas, breaking them into objectives, and using layers to gather targeted feedback from peers, faculty, and community experts.

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Turns abstract assignments into structured, documented ventures. Automatically generates a portfolio of student work and provides grant-ready data on engagement and impact.

2. The Community Partnership Engine

Supercharge CELTC's work with local nonprofits, co-ops, and government partners.

  • How it works: Community partners post specific tasks they’d like help with as "Task Cards” on Needpedia. Students find opportunities matching their skills quickly and easily using “skill tags”, and the platform tracks their contributions in the “/impact” section in their profile, creating a clear record of trust for future engagements.

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Moves beyond static internship lists to a dynamic, skill-based matching system. Deepens community ties, (and reach), and demonstrates tangible, documented contributions to regional partners.

3. Staying Constructive Despite Controversy

A structured method for students and community groups to move from talk to action on polarized issues.

  • How it works: A student or partner shares an idea for something important but polarized, then creates an NVC (Nonviolent Communication) Layers to facilitate discussion in a productive way. *Note that AI will be incorporated to only allow content which does not violate the rules of NVC in NVC layers, to ensure they will be productive and healthy spaces for all involved.

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Provides a real-world tool for the "transformational change" CELTC teaches. By creating an archive for all people’s ideas we create a catalyst for collaboration which invariably causes people to meet those outside their existing social and political divides. By facilitating interactions that can span over time in an organized and productive way, we begin facilitating the depolarization of individuals en mass.

4. The Action-Research Project

A formal partnership to study the process of collaboration itself.

  • How it works: CELTC faculty and students use Needpedia as a living lab. Example research questions: "Can the NVC system reduce online hostility?" or "Can Identity Layers increase participation from under-represented groups?"

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Generates publishable data, supports grant applications, and gives students unparalleled hands-on research experience in civic tech and social psychology.

5. The Regional Hub ("Cascadia Commons")

Position CELTC as the central convener for civic innovation in the Pacific Northwest.

  • How it works: CELTC curates a dedicated space on Needpedia for regional challenges. You onboard community partners, feature student-led projects, and host digital "solution sprints" for local issues.

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Establishes Evergreen as the go-to leader for participatory problem-solving in the region, attracting students, partners, and funders.

6. The "Civic Tech" Practicum

Bridge technology and social change for CS and MPA students.

  • How it works: Students contribute directly to Needpedia's open-source codebase as a capstone or practicum project—improving accessibility, building new features, or analyzing platform data.

  • The Payoff for CELTC: Offers students a rare opportunity to gain resume-worthy experience in mission-driven software development, directly applying their skills for social good.

How to Start (No Heavy Lifting Required)

  • Option A: The Single-Class Pilot. One faculty member uses it for one course project next semester. We handle the training.

  • Option B: The Focused Research Sprint. A faculty-student team picks one research question and uses the platform to test it for a quarter.

  • Option C: The Community Challenge. We partner with one of your existing community partners to run a 6-week "problem-solving sprint" on a specific issue, facilitated by a CELTC student.

The bottom line: New and advanced tools for organizing we’ve been developing are ready. You have the real-world challenges and passionate people. Let's put them together and see what we can accomplish.
