Tiffany’s Idea


This is Tiffany. She noticed the sex ed program at her school is basically useless. It doesn't even cover consent. She has ideas for making it better, but alone she's just 1 person. A few days go by, a teacher or two applaud her for caring so much, but no one offers to actually help her, and she starts to give up. After all, it doesn't matter if her idea's good if there's no real way of implementing it. Even worse, she starts realizing there's actually no point in knowing or thinking about anything if she can't actually change anything, and she begins to lose hope in not only the future, but in herself as well.

Fortunately she eventually hears about people creating a place for all ideas people have, for any problem, with any-thing. So she decides to give it a try and posts her idea for a better sex ed program there. The archive itself doesn't have a lot of users yet so she knows it's not going to create an audience for her idea all on its own, but that doesn't actually matter, because now that her idea has a URL attached, she can link people to it. She no longer has to repeat herself, she can instead say it once, add links and other useful stuff, and even gather support there. She shows the post to her parents and they help her edit a few things to better appeal to the sensibilities of other parents and teachers. These edits were mostly just basic things like improving the way it's written. Her dad pointed out a concern or two that other parents might have, and her mom pointed out that win or lose it's really cool that she cares so much about this issue that she wants to make a difference. That she's proud of her. Her dad's secretly proud too but doesn't want to see her get too disappointed if things don't work out.

Now that the idea's more refined, she thinks about how she could appeal to the other students. She makes a short video asking people if they knew certain things about consent, and how valuable that might've been to them had they learned it sooner. For instance, the fact that consent isn't just about sexuality, it pertains to everything else in human existence. Including the ability to not get exploited. -Financially, spiritually, or romantically.


It's actually a pretty good video, she posts it to Tiktok and Pinterest and it gets a fair number of views. A bunch of girls respond to it, talking about the bad experiences they've had and how they felt let down by the current program. Most of her classmates still don't see the video but now she has a video explaining her idea, a small following of supporters, and a link to actually go work on it in the archive. -Which begins getting activity. A girl from her school named Katie said she's down to help in the wiki posts comment section. One of the "objectives" listed in the archive mentions she's gonna need people to help spread the word, and Katy said she can help with that. Now she's posting links to Tiffany's video and archive post, and even gets a friend to help. Eventually the community of supporters grows to a few dozen people. Hitting "Follow" on the archive post means you get notifications every time someone does something to help, such as when another student, Brian, comments that he wants to do a media project around Tiffany's idea. For the next week he can be seen randomly interviewing classmates about consent, and testing to see if people can answer questions everyone their age should all already know. Each time Brian does a video it touches a different social group and draws all their friends in. Each time they fail he has another funny reaction video to post and before long Tiffany's idea has hundreds of supporters. 


The teachers who simply applauded Tiffany initially, realize the situation has evolved, and that this is a powerful opportunity to teach about civic engagement, and to improve the sex ed program. One shares it with the principal who despite being overworked, is immediately excited.

The next PTA meeting features a section where Tiffany's idea is formally presented to the entire community, and voted on. Most parents are immediately on board but some of the more religious ones have concerns.
